# Spoke: how it compares to other group-ride apps

Short research notes from November 2026. Use them to explain your design choices in your submission or in an interview.

## What's already out there

| App | What it does well | What it doesn't do for a nervous first-timer |
|---|---|---|
| **Strava** | Huge community. It recently added joining communities and setting up group rides in the app. | Built around tracking and performance. Free users only get some club features. Reviewers flag that workout locations are public by default, which is a privacy and safety worry. |
| **Komoot** | Route planning, plus scheduling group rides and events. Popular with clubs and race organizers. | Assumes you already know routes and riding. Little since the 2025 ownership change suggests a shift toward beginners. |
| **PopRide** | Host one-time or recurring rides, RSVP, comment, find rides nearby, filter by difficulty. | A general listing app. It doesn't explain group-ride rules to newcomers. |
| **Draft Ride** | Browse rides by pace, route and crew. | Pace-first browsing suits people who already know their speed. |
| **Cyql** | Club management for organizers. | Made for the club, not the newcomer. |
| **ROUVY / Zwift** | Indoor no-drop group rides with "sweepers" and rookie rides. | Virtual rides, not real streets. |

## What good group rides do (from cycling guides)

- **No-drop:** someone pedals back with slower riders, and a named **sweep** rides at the back.
- **Pace bands:** rides are labeled by speed. A 12–14 mph band is often called "beginner/social".
- **The advertised pace is an average** over the ride, not counting stops.

## How Spoke answers that

| Gap in the market | What Spoke does |
|---|---|
| Newcomers don't know group-ride rules | **Every** ride is no-drop, and each ride names its **sweep rider** and explains "How this ride works". |
| Pace numbers mean nothing to beginners | Each ride shows a **pace level** (Starter, Easy, Relaxed, Steady) and says "average while moving, not counting stops". |
| Public location data scares people | The Join screen promises: **only the leader sees your name and bike; rides and locations are never public.** |
| "Is this ride right for me?" | **Picked for you**: 3 questions, then matching rides are marked and sorted first. |
| Questions you're shy to ask | **Ask Spoke**: an AI assistant that knows every ride and your bookings. It answers "what do I bring?" or "I don't own a bike". |
| Showing up alone | A **ride buddy** option pairs you with another first-timer. |
| Full rides | **Waitlist** with your position. |
| Getting there | **Directions** to the meeting point and a **bike-share** option with the nearest dock. |
| Recurring rides | Each ride shows its schedule, for example "Every Saturday". |

**One-line pitch:** Strava and Komoot are built for people who already ride. Spoke is built for the person who's never ridden with a group and is nervous to try.
